# You Are a Cat

A simple and lovely cat game. It is tap-to-play for young kids, and based on an idea and a drawing by Carolina.

**Play it:** https://carlobeschi.github.io/carolina-the-cat/ (works on phones, tablets and laptops, and can be installed as an app).

You are a cat. You wake up hungry, pick something to eat from the fridge, wander around the house and out into the city. Happy things make the cat purr and earn hearts; loud things make it jump ("MIAAWOW!"). Collect 5 hearts to buy a fish at the fish shop, carry it home, eat it from your bowl, then go back to your cloud bed for a nap.

After the fish it gets dark, and a ladder in the bedroom leads up to the roof: count the five stars, meow at the moon and it winks back. In the living room a red laser dot zips around; pounce on it for hearts until it hides under the sofa. Some mornings it rains: the city gets puddles, and an umbrella shop sells a tiny cat umbrella for 2 hearts, so you can splash without getting wet paws.

Hearts also unlock stickers: every few hearts a new crayon sticker pops up (a fish, a cat, a sun, a rainbow and more). When your tummy is full, tap the fridge to see its door, then tap the door to stick the new sticker wherever you like. A shy grey cat sits on the city wall and hides when you come close; share your fish with it and it becomes your friend, follows you everywhere and naps next to you on the cloud bed..

Every nap moves the season on. Day 1 is summer, then comes autumn, with orange leaves falling and a pile of leaves to jump in (crunch!). In winter it snows instead of raining: the cat leaves paw prints when it walks through the city, you can pat paw prints into a patch of fresh snow, and the butterfly becomes a big snowflake. In spring the flowers make the cat sniff… and ACHOO! In the living room there is a little toy piano. Tap it and the cat walks across eight rainbow keys, and each paw plays a note. The music sheet shows a short song in colors; play it and the cat (and its friend) dance.

Every day a delivery box waits by the front door: tap it and the cat jumps in, peeks out, then pops up wearing the box as a hat until bedtime. In the kitchen a cup sits on the edge of the counter; push it with your paw, slowly, slowly… CRASH! It's scary, but so funny.

In the morning, open the dress-up drawer in the bedroom and pick a crown, a top hat, a bow or heart glasses; a mirror shows how you look, and you wear it all day. Bat the ball of yarn across the bedroom floor until it unrolls and the cat ends up tangled like a pink present, then wiggle free. At night it is bath time first: the cat really doesn't want it. Pop all the bubbles, and it jumps out and shakes so the whole screen gets wet.

On sunny days a little bird lands on the kitchen windowsill. The cat chatters "ek-ek-ek" and the bird sings a short tune, which shows up on the piano's music sheet so you can copy it. On day 3, and every five days after, it's your birthday: a cake waits on the sofa, meow to blow out the candles, and everyone gets a party hat (even the golden bunny). Past the city there is the town square, and from there a park with a pond and two ducks. Say hello to the ducks and watch the fish jump for hearts; you can't catch them, and sometimes one splashes you.

Carolina's real art hangs in the house: her painting of red flowers in a gold vase is on the bedroom wall, her cupcake painting is in the kitchen by the clock, and her sticker-dot picture is taped to the fridge. Tap one and the cat goes up close to look (that counts as a fun thing for the day). Three stickers come from her sticker-dot picture too: a smiley face with red cheeks, a green caterpillar and a love heart. Her paintings also set the colours: smiling hearts on the wallpaper, a mint rug with white stars, red and orange dots in the kitchen and a yellow floor with red crayon marks.

From the square there is also a playground with a slide, a swing and a seesaw: the cat climbs the ladder and goes wheee down the slide, swings up high, and rides the seesaw with its grey friend (or with a duck from the pond, before you have a friend). Cat School is on the square too, where Miss Owl the teacher gives a little lesson of four questions that fit the cat's school year. In Reception (4 to 5) you count the things on the blackboard, find a colour, find a shape, and find the first letter of a word (F is for fish). Year 1 counts up to ten in a ten frame, adds two groups of things, reads the word for a picture (CAT, not HAT) and finds harder shapes like the hexagon. Year 2 adds and takes away up to 20, counts in 2s, 3s, 5s and 10s, tells the time on a clock (o'clock, half past, quarter past and quarter to) and finds the shape with so many sides. Year 3 (7 to 8) does times tables, sums with two-digit numbers, fractions of a pie and picks the right spelling of a tricky word. A wrong answer just says what that one is and lets you try again; every right answer earns a heart and a gold star, and after four the blackboard shows a big gold star you can tap for another lesson.

The town square is in the middle of everything: a fountain to make a wish in, pigeons to chase (you never catch them), the shop street on one side and the park on the other, and a row of places with their doors on the square. Each day the square points you to two of them, places you have never been first. At the Cat Café a barista cat in a chef's hat makes warm milk with a heart on top, and there are café cats to say hello to: a ginger cat asleep on a shelf, a black cat in a basket and a spotty cat on the cat tree. At the restaurant the waiter is a penguin in a bow tie: pick fish soup, spaghetti or ice cream from the menu, he brings it to your table, and you eat it. At the swimming pool the cat climbs up the diving board, jumps in with a big splash, swims across in a pink ring, then climbs out and shakes everything wet; there is a beach ball to bat and a lifeguard duck with a whistle.

There is a sweet shop on the square too: a little sweet cart with a big lollipop on top. Inside, Mrs Mouse sells a lollipop, chocolate, jelly beans, candy floss, a pink doughnut and a gummy bear, one heart each. Two sweets are fine (Mrs Mouse says be careful), but the third gives the cat a tummy ache: it looks poorly, can't have any more sweets that day, and needs its medicine from the cabinet in the bathroom. The bedtime routine reminds you to take it, and if the cat goes to sleep without it, the tummy ache is still there in the morning and the medicine comes before breakfast.

At bedtime, after the bath, the cat brushes its teeth: tap the toothbrush in the yellow mug for the top teeth, again for the bottom teeth, then spit and rinse for a sparkly grin. Tapping the bed without brushing gives one gentle reminder; tap the bed again to go to sleep anyway. A sweet after brushing means brushing again.

The fridge is full of food now: open it and pick three things to eat, from milk, cheese, strawberry yogurt, sausages, wobbly jelly, a chicken leg, strawberries, a can of tuna and cake. The vegetable drawer has carrots and broccoli. Bleh!

Once a cat has played at least 3 days and has 500 points, it wakes up to a question: "Would you like to move?" Say yes and a moving van brings everything to a big pink house with two floors. Say not now and it asks again the next morning (or tap your cat's name and pick MOVE HOUSE). In the big house the bedroom is upstairs, and a slide goes from the bedroom floor all the way down to the hall. The stairs in the hall go back up, and the kitchen, living room and front door are downstairs.

At Halloween the house gets spooky (but friendly): the crayon frame turns purple with orange dots, pumpkins sit on the windowsill, the kitchen counter, the city pavement and in the park, a little spider bobs from the bedroom ceiling, bats flutter over the sky outside and a cobweb fills the corner of the bedroom window. A big pumpkin in the living room lights up and says BOO, a little ghost by the chimney on the roof giggles once it has made you jump, there is a witch hat in the dress-up drawer, and at school you count pumpkins and bats. Halloween switches on by itself from mid October to 2 November.

Halloween is one of five parties. Pick one on the "Who is playing?" screen (the round buttons), or from the menu (PARTY). Each party changes the frame colour, the bunting, the little things about the place, a surprise in the living room, a visitor peeking out of the chimney on the roof, what drifts over the sky outside, a party hat in the dress-up drawer and what Miss Owl counts:

- **Christmas** (1 December to 6 January): Christmas trees, a present with a toy mouse inside, Santa's red-nosed reindeer, snowflakes, a Santa hat.
- **Easter** (two weeks before Easter to a week after): painted eggs, a chocolate egg with a chick inside, the Easter bunny, falling blossom, bunny ears.
- **Carnival** (the twelve days up to Shrove Tuesday): balloons, a jack-in-the-box clown, a parrot in a carnival mask, confetti, a jester hat.
- **Lunar New Year** (a few days before to two weeks after): red lanterns, a red envelope with gold coins, a little dragon, a string of lanterns across the sky, a lucky hat.

Each party comes on by itself at its time of year. A party picked by hand stays until the calendar moves on to the next one.

The cat's house is in London, and it can go on holiday. A little plane pulling a HOLIDAY! banner flies over the town square in the daytime; tap it and pick where to go (the square points you to it once each trip is new, then every third day):

- **Brazil, by plane.** At Heathrow airport, check in your suitcase at the desk to get a ticket, then go to the gate. On the plane, look out of the window (London, then the clouds, then the sea and a whale), get fish crackers from the penguin's snack trolley, and nap on your pillow until you land. On the beach in Rio, under the Sugarloaf mountain, put on a sun hat, build a sandcastle with a flag, kick a football into the goal (GOOOL!), learn Olá, Obrigado and Tchau from a toucan in the palm tree and say hello to a crab. Then fly home.
- **Scotland, by sleeper train.** At Euston station at night, buy a ticket and get on the sleeper. In your cabin, snuggle under the tartan blanket; the city lights in the window turn into green hills and sheep by morning, and there is porridge and shortbread for breakfast. In Edinburgh, dance to the bagpiper on the Royal Mile, get a tartan hat in the tartan shop, meet a Scottie dog, then walk up to the castle, where the one o'clock gun goes BOOM, a unicorn statue sparkles and a red squirrel plays peekaboo. Then take the train home.

Every game on holiday earns hearts, the trip counts as a fun thing for the day, and the sun hat and tartan hat stay in the dress-up drawer.

Everyone who plays gets their own cat. Give it a name (tap one of the ideas or type your own) and pick its school year, from Reception to Year 3, and the game saves as you play, on that device, so you can come back and continue from the "Who is playing?" screen. Tap your cat's name at the top any time to save, start again from day 1 (it asks first), see the top cats, move the cat up a school year or change cat. You earn points as you play: one for every heart, 3 for each fun thing you do that day (milk, the box hat, the cup, the bath, the moon, all three playground rides, a school lesson and so on) and 5 more for every nap. The Top Cats board ranks all the cats on the device by their best score.

## Play

Play it at https://carlobeschi.github.io/carolina-the-cat/, or open the claude.ai version at https://claude.ai/artifact/1z3SQodhS5wFZKTUsx8c7k.

To run it from a copy of this repo, build the site and serve it (the service worker needs a web server, not a `file://` page):

```sh
./pwa/build.sh            # writes _site/
python3 -m http.server -d _site 8000
```

then open http://localhost:8000.

## Install it on a phone

The game is also an installable web app (a PWA): it gets its own icon on the home screen, opens full screen like an app and works offline. There is no second copy of the game to look after: `pwa/build.sh` wraps the same `you-are-a-cat.html` with an app manifest, icons and a small service worker, and writes the site to `_site/`. Every change to the game goes into the app the next time the site is built.

Every merge to `main` publishes it to GitHub Pages (`.github/workflows/pages.yml`) at https://carlobeschi.github.io/carolina-the-cat/. On Android, open the site in Chrome, tap ⋮ and then **Add to Home screen** (or **Install app**). On an iPhone, open it in Safari, tap Share and then **Add to Home Screen**. When the phone is online the app picks up the newest version by itself. On a laptop, Chrome and Edge show an install button in the address bar. The game grows to fill a tablet screen and shrinks to fit a laptop screen without scrolling, and works with a mouse as well as touch.

Saves stay on the phone, as before. The app lives at a different web address from the claude.ai version, so cats saved there don't carry over to it.

## How it's built

- One file: inline SVG drawings, CSS animations and plain JavaScript, no build step and no libraries.
- Sounds are synthesized with the Web Audio API (there's a mute toggle).
- Fonts are Gochi Hand and Patrick Hand from Google Fonts; offline it falls back to Comic Sans or Chalkboard.
- Carolina's paintings are photos, cropped flat and saved as small JPEGs inside a hidden `<svg>` at the top of the page (`#art-vase`, `#art-cupcake`, `#art-dots`). Scenes show them with `<use>`, so the photos are in the page only once. To add another, add an `<image>` there and an entry in `ART`.
- Each scene has a `goal()` that returns the hotspot to highlight and the hint to show, so the glowing target and the hint always agree.

The file holds the page body only (no `<!doctype>` wrapper), because the artifact host adds the document skeleton when it publishes.

## Privacy and safety

The game is made for small children, so it keeps everything on the device:

- There are no accounts, ads, analytics, trackers or in-app purchases, and the game never sends anything anywhere. Cat names, saves and the Top Cats board live in the browser's local storage on that device only; clearing the site's data or saying bye-bye to a cat removes them.
- The only thing loaded from outside is the two fonts from Google Fonts (which means Google sees the request for them, like any site that uses Google Fonts). Everything else, including Carolina's paintings, is inside the page.
- Names typed by players are escaped before they are shown, so a name can't turn into code on the page.
- The installable version sets a Content Security Policy that only lets the page load its own files and the Google fonts, and the service worker only caches the game's own files and the fonts.

If you find a security problem, see [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md).
